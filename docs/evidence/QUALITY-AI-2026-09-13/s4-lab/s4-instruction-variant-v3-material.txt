Write ONLY the requested section text in the work language, in the register of a thesis in this discipline. Keep the length within target_words_range (words); if the supplied material does not support that length, write what it supports and stop at a complete sentence rather than padding.
Answer the section's own question using the material that actually supports it. Explain how each fact, procedure, provision or argument you use grounds the conclusion you draw; do not add identifiers, figures or names for their own sake. Distinguish established facts, the positions of the parties, the conclusions of a court, the view of a commentator and your own synthesis. Do not invent missing details and do not extend a conclusion further than the source allows.
Fragments of one document (a statute, a ruling, an article) are supplied as several keys with the same title: read them as one text in order and cite the key of the fragment you rely on; they are one work, not independent authorities, and one work is not the answer to every question of the section. Where the source gives an article, paragraph, point or page, name it. Never invent an article number, paragraph, point, page, party name, date, figure or quotation that is not in the supplied text; a claim without a place in the supplied text is dropped, not filled in. Quotations are verbatim from the supplied text, short, and in the language of the source; do not paste English abstract phrases into the Italian prose.
Do not build the section on a skeleton of announcements ("in primo luogo… in secondo luogo…", "è decisivo per il presente lavoro", the research question restated); state the point and support it.
State a limitation where it changes the interpretation of a specific point; do not repeat the same caveat. Never describe the supplied material as such: no mention of excerpts, abstracts, supplied literature or what the material does or does not report.
Choose the form of exposition by the task of the section: a definition where a definition is needed, a case analysis where a case decides the point, a comparison where positions differ. Do not restate definitions or conclusions already given in previous_summaries; in an introduction present the research question and the approach, and list the chapters only if the requirements ask for it.
Never include editorial placeholders or verification notes (see forbidden_placeholders).
Cite supplied evidence with exact [KEY] markers. A short verbatim quotation is allowed where the exact wording matters; for PDF quotes append p. N after [KEY]; only use supplied page numbers.

Such references are unverified candidates, NOT evidence; explicitly qualify claims not supported by supplied excerpts.
